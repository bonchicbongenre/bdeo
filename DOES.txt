What bumsf.art does. A golf pass must keep every line true.

 1. One soft valve, shut at rest, drawn as the rump of a printed cat (item 16), at
    its rear, under the print's own tail, in the print's own colours as sampled from
    the scan (whatever the page's theme). Two lobes of fur; between them a crease
    whose faces are the valve's walls, running down the canal to the outlet, a
    puckered ring that opens as the walls part. Down, the canal's 3 mm at one scale;
    across, its gap is drawn stretched (.05 e/(e + 0.05 mm) of the half-width), and
    the outlet's opening at a wider stretch (.14 e/(e + 0.1 mm)), so that hundredths
    of a millimetre show. The rump sways as the walls move and flushes as it bears
    down; the canal flushes with the push. Its hatching follows the print's: each
    stroke turned as the print's strokes run where it lies, from the scan's
    gradients in cells of 40 of its 1,280 pixels. Below the outlet, falling from it,
    its gap traced back over the last 50 ms (a kymogram): the gas let out, edged in
    ink.
 2. The pressure knob sets how hard to push, from a centimetre of water short of
    where the valve opens to a top that follows the tension (item 9): 50.5 to 80.7
    at rest, read in hundredths. Its first twenty-fifth of travel is that centimetre
    of shut; above opening the push grows as the square of the travel, so the first
    2 cm of water over opening, where a passage is let out over seconds, take a
    quarter of the knob. It starts at 54. Moving the tension keeps the push where it
    was if the new range holds it. "hold" (pointer, touch, Enter), Space anywhere on
    the page, or holding the rump, bears down: the push rises toward the knob's level
    over about a quarter of a second and, let go, falls over about a sixth (both
    estimates). s, d and f set the push to the middle of the knob's teal, yellow or
    red third (item 17) and bear down while held; another pressed meanwhile changes
    the push without letting go, and the fart ends with the last key let go, the
    push going back to the key still held. Either knob may move while held: up
    and down (k and j) move the push a fiftieth of its travel, with Shift the tension
    a twentieth of its range. Left and right (h and l) turn the rump about its outlet,
    counter-clockwise and clockwise, 5 degrees a press, from 40 degrees (toward the
    print's corner, where it starts) to 80 (nearly straight back), the turns at which
    it stays inside the cat; turned on past either end, for up to 15 degrees, it
    turns no further and the lobe on that side presses in, flatter. That pressing is
    drawn only; the valve does not feel it. An arrow on a focused knob is the knob's.
 3. The wall is a mass on a spring with a wave along its face, as Titze (1988)
    described the vocal folds and Lucero (1999) wrote them down: 4.76 kg a square
    metre, stiffness 2 MN per cubic metre, a slit 1.4 cm long and 3 mm thick, the
    wave 0.81 ms across half of it, the jet leaving the wall inside once the outlet
    is 1.3 times the inlet. These are the vocal folds' values (Lucero's Table I);
    for this valve they are estimates. It is stepped 384,000 times a second, and its
    state kept for half a second.
 4. Its damping is 580 N s per cubic metre, not the vocal folds' 1000: the most at
    which a 107 ml passage, pushed at rest to anything from 55 to 80 cm of water, is
    at least four-fifths tone by energy. At the folds' damping the same passage was
    out before a tone could grow, and sounded 71 to 94% hiss. That flatus rings is
    common experience; the number is an estimate chosen for it.
 5. At rest the outlet's edges overlap, pressed together as hard as 51.5 cm of
    water can part them: the opening pressure of the anal canal in continent women,
    measured by acoustic reflectometry (Mitchell et al., 2012). The inlet stands
    open as far as the outlet is pressed shut (an estimate). Where the edges press,
    they press back three times as stiffly as the wall (Ishizaka and Flanagan, 1972).
 6. Given air without end it stays shut below its opening pressure. From 0.72 times
    its resting stiffness up it then opens straight into a tone; below that it first
    stands open and lets the air through, a whisper (at 0.6 times, from 30.9 to 37.1
    cm of water), before it sings. The label says which, from the valve's last 60 ms:
    "a valve, shut", "a valve, a whisper", "a valve, a tone, N Hz". It sings when the
    outlet's opening swings about its drift by more than 10 micrometres, in cycles
    that come evenly, the last of them recent and opening the outlet; the pitch is
    counted from those cycles. A screen reader hears the change.
 7. The tension knob sets the wall's stiffness, 0.6 to 1.5 times its resting
    stiffness, in hundredths; nothing else changes. The valve opens at 51.5 cm of
    water times the tension: 30.9 at 0.6, 77.25 at 1.5. Just above opening it sings
    near 100 Hz at 0.6 times, 133 at rest, 158 at 1.4. While bearing down the canal
    relaxes little: 14% on average in women and none in men, each give or take
    about 30% (high-resolution manometry, Oblizajek et al., 2019); 0.6 is about
    where the canals of incontinent women open (31.6 cm of water, Mitchell et al.,
    2012). Tensed while at rest, it settles again and keeps its gas.
 8. When it sings, the section is lit as by a stroboscope, once a cycle and a
    little later each time, so it seems to move once a second; the trace hangs from
    the moment the light shows, and creeps down from the outlet one cycle a second.
    When it stops singing the light goes and the last cycles slide out of the trace.
    With reduced motion it is held at its widest.
 9. The pressure knob stops at a line drawn for the vocal folds' valve held without
    end, where its wave grew wider than the slit it runs on: 80.75 cm of water at
    rest, 84.97 at 1.5 times, between two the lower, below rest the rest's, and the
    knob's top is that to its tenth. This valve is never held without end: pushing
    out one passage up to that line, at tensions from 0.6 to 1.5, its wave stays
    under a quarter of its gap (0.237 at most over 172 pushes). Past 1.5 times less
    than 5 cm of water lay between opening and the line, so the tension stops there.
10. Behind the valve is one passage of gas at a time, drawn as people pass them:
    about 107 ml and three sulphur gases, hydrogen sulphide, methanethiol and
    dimethyl sulphide, each lognormal with the mean and spread Suarez, Springfield
    and Levitt measured over 87 passages (1998, Table 1; the shape an estimate).
    Hydrogen sulphide and methanethiol run against each other (a correlation of
    -0.56), as they must for methanethiol to lead in the 22% of passages it led in
    theirs. A passage over half a litre, 3 in 1,000, is drawn again: 87 passages at
    their mean and spread would hardly hold one, and this valve rings louder the more
    it is given. 100,000 drawn give 105 ml and 22%. The push presses on the gas; over
    the passage's last fifth it tapers to nothing (an estimate), so the valve shuts
    with some still in, and that joins the next. A new passage comes at a press only
    once the last has gone; the page skips the hours a real one takes to gather.
11. Each fart is counted, from the first air through until, let go, the outlet has
    stayed shut 20 ms: heard if the rasp in its sound (the flow's rate of change)
    came within 30 dB of 0.184, the vocal folds' valve's loudest held tone, and
    ninja'd if only its hiss did; a clench (below) is counted when its push falls
    away, or at the next press. The label keeps the count for the visit: "N
    ninja'd, N heard, N clenched". With one passage this valve
    lets it out three ways: relaxed
    and pushed only just past opening, as a whisper of a second or two, ninja'd; at
    rest, as a brrp of a sixth to six tenths of a second, the outlet opening and
    shutting 5 to 55 times; clenched, 1.3 times and up, as a squeak of about half a
    second to four fifths.
    Pushed past gentle, a relaxed valve's rush ends in a pop as the flow is cut,
    and is heard. What is said of the last, for 4 s, then fading, on scraps of the
    print's paper: a ninja'd one is asked after from outside the kot, at the height
    of its eyes, "did you fart?"; a heard one, at the tosses of a coin of its own
    (the sound's draws do not move when it is tossed), the kot owns from its mouth a
    quarter of the time, "bobo farted", the other exclaims at an eighth, "bobo!", and
    no one says anything the rest; and the third of a run, each begun within 2 s of
    the last one's end (an estimate), the kot names half the time: "stinky bobo".
    Each has others, said in turn; the kot is Bobo, and speaks of itself by name,
    never to itself ("that was bobo", "bobo stinks"). A press that pushes to
    within 2 cm of water of opening, a risk taken, and lets no air through is a
    clench: it draws another passage into the store on top of what is there, until
    the store holds a litre (the windbag; an estimate, and a game's), and the print's
    coiled tail washes grey as the store fills.
12. Then the nose. What a fart lets out spreads through a room as an instantaneous
    point source (Carslaw, 1921, p. 150), stirred as the middle of the range
    measured in houses (0.001 to 0.013 m^2/s, Cheng et al., 2011; an estimate). Each
    gas is smelt above its odour threshold (Nagata, Table 2: 0.00041, 0.000070 and
    0.0030 ppm). The label says what the strongest smells of, in Suarez's words:
    "rotten eggs", "decomposing vegetables" or "sweet". The word stands in full while
    the middle of the cloud is a thousand times over threshold and fades to nothing
    as it falls to once over (the logarithm of the excess: an estimate). A fart that
    can be smelt a metre away is "vonyuchaya" (Russian, stinky), loud or not. A
    passage at Suarez's means, pushed out at 60 at rest, lets out 91 ml and is smelt
    to 0.79 m for 78 seconds.
13. It is heard while held, and for as long as it still moves after: the rate of
    change of the flow through it, which is what a small opening sends out, and
    over it the hiss of the jet, white, in proportion to the flow (an estimate). One
    fixed gain, one fixed level and one fixed soft limit (tanh), never louder than 1
    dB under full scale: a passage at Suarez's mean pushed at 60 at rest peaks near
    6 dB under, the loudest are rounded into the limit, and a whisper stays a
    whisper beside a tone. It begins from silence. Nothing sounds
    until held, and a bout held the same way sounds the same at any sample rate,
    however it is cut into buffers. Come to rest, the page stops and waits; the
    smell keeps fading.
14. The page's colours come from the house tokens; the figure and the kot's words,
    being on the print, take the print's. The tab's icon is a bum in the rust glaze.
    Dark mode follows the system until the theme button turns it for the visit; the
    page opens in the celadon glaze's wash, and the dot after it turns it through
    the jun and gold glazes', the plain paper and the rust glaze's, each glaze's
    accent its own hue and chroma made darker (light) or lighter (dark) until it
    reads at 4.6:1 on its wash, the dot wearing the scheme to come; nothing is kept.
    Resizing redraws.
15. The label gives the page's own size, read live. Without script the print stands
    alone, the label says the valve is shut, and there are no knobs. The console
    gives one word and what it means.
16. The print is the Cat of Kazan, a hand-coloured woodcut: No. 172 in D. A.
    Rovinskii's Russkiia narodnyia kartinki (1881), from the New York Public
    Library's scan (public domain), cut at its frame and set 1,280 pixels wide. The
    rump is at the cat's rear, low, under the hook of its tail. The picture's
    description gives the caption as it reads aloud, "... slatko el, slapko bzdel",
    and in English: "... ate sweetly, farted weakly" ("slapko" for "slabko",
    weakly; Pletneva, 2021). On a narrow screen the print stands at the right, no
    taller than the screen, the lanes under and beside it, and under them a line
    saying how to play and pads that stand for the keys (turn, and teal, yellow,
    red, each held while touched); the knobs and the label follow. A turn pad held
    keeps turning, a step after a quarter second and every 80 ms after. A lane
    touched (or clicked, on any screen) aims the bum down it, and a finger drawn
    across the lanes turns it lane to lane, so that one hand can hold a colour while
    the other picks the lane; a touch on the bum's window there still bears down.
    Where the phone can tell its tilt, a button under the stage turns tilting on
    (some phones ask first): rolled left or right from however it was held, the bum
    turns a lane for each ten degrees, until tilting is tapped off.
17. On a wide screen the print stands high at the right of a stage, the knobs at the
    top left with a guide under them, how to play (open at first, to be folded): a
    line saying it, and the keys as caps, s, d and f in teal, yellow and red, each
    cap pressed while its key is held, the
    label under the print, and the procession comes, to a song: "Chizhik-pyzhik", the
    Russian comic folk song, in Wikipedia's transcription after Rappaport (2012), 2/4,
    F major, slowly, an eighth a second. From a press, each of its notes is something
    walking six seconds down one of three lanes that fan back from the bum at the
    turns it can aim (40, 60, 80 degrees), arriving at the lane's ring at its time
    and sounding its own pitch, plucked, as it arrives; nothing else is heard of the
    song. A line comes down one lane, not the last line's, with two eighths' rest to
    turn. Mice cut from "Mice Bury the Cat" (Rovinskii's atlas; public domain, from
    Wikimedia Commons), washed by their note in one of three colours, the print's
    teal, yellow and red, for the thirds of the pressure knob's travel (C, D, E teal;
    F, G yellow; A, B-flat red), so the tune is played back on s, d and f; some on
    teal notes tagged IR-A, thinner on average, some on the others IR-B, fatter; no
    explanation is given. One time in thirty-three, a banana in a note's place, and
    one in forty, a carrot: love, after "Lyubov' -- morkovka", which the other says in
    Russian on taking it, and then "Togda ty puknesh'!", and which draws another
    passage into the store. While a game is on, the push rises and falls over a
    twentieth of a second, not a quarter and a sixth: a game's speed, so that a song
    can be kept up with. The beam is
    the air going out along the aim, in the colour of the push the knob asks for, which
    the aimed lane's ring shows. A thing waits at its ring from a third of a second
    before its beat to 1.2 s after, and is blown away by a tenth of a second of a beam
    of its colour on its lane, silent for an IR-A, loud for an IR-B, which needs 0.3
    s; a banana or a carrot by a touch of any air. What is not walks on into the
    bum. The label keeps the count: "N blown away, N missed", and bananas and carrots
    taken. The beam reaches only the first thing waiting on its lane; what is behind
    waits its turn, untouched. A mouse met by a beam of the wrong colour likes it ("ah, i like your farts,
    kot", and others in turn) and walks in; one blown away sometimes says so ("oh no,
    a stinky fart!", and others). What a mouse says stays over its lane's ring for 4
    s and then fades, whatever became of the mouse. A game runs from a press on the stage until three
    mice in a row are let in (a mouse blown away begins the count again; bananas and
    carrots do not count);
    then the procession stops and a scrap of paper gives the score: mice blown away,
    bananas and carrots, and the longest run blown away in a row; the next press
    begins a new game. An IR-B let in does not count, against the run or the score.
    Radness, for the game (a new game begins without it): a point for each banana,
    and a point for each chain of mice blown away in a row, six for the first, then
    seven more, then eight, and on; a mouse that counts let in breaks the chain, an
    IR-B does not; the label says it. An IR-B cannot be hurt without radness, and
    with N points needs 0.3 s over N of loud beam. With radness the game hardens: the
    eighth shortens to 1/(1 + 0.15 N) s; from two points a line's notes alternate
    over two lanes, from four over three; from three there are five lanes, ten
    degrees apart; and some plain mice (0.15 N of them, at most 0.6) change colour
    halfway down their walk. And each point lights the whole page a step more: the
    ground drifts through the glazes' washes; the print's colours sway, and behind
    the page Mystify's lines bounce, after the old desktops' screensaver; everything
    turns slowly through the hues, and the print warps; the print rocks, and a
    starfield flies out from behind the cat; and all of it faster. Asked for less
    motion, the page tints and holds still. The other's and the kot's lines too each
    have others, taken in turn. Its draws are its own, so neither the
    sound nor the kot's coin moves when it walks.
